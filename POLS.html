A Bijection Between Mutually Orthogonal and Mutually Pseudo Orthogonal Latin Squares

Shahab Faruqi
Department of Mathematics
National Defence Academy
Pune 411023, India
shahabfaruqi@gmail.com
Abstract. We establish an explicit bijection between ordered families of mutually orthogonal Latin squares and ordered families of mutually pseudo-orthogonal Latin squares of the same order. As a consequence, the existence of $m$ mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order $n$ is equivalent to the existence of $m$ mutually pseudo-orthogonal Latin squares of order $n$.

1. Introduction

A Latin square of order $n$ is an $n\times n$ array $L=(L(i,j))$ on the symbol set $S$, where $|S|=n$, such that every symbol $s\in S$ occurs exactly once in each row and exactly once in each column. Throughout this paper, $[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$. Two Latin squares $P,Q$ of order $n$ are said to be orthogonal if the ordered pairs $(P(i,j),Q(i,j))$ are all distinct as $(i,j)$ ranges over $[n]^2$. A family of Latin squares is called a family of mutually orthogonal Latin squares (MOLS) if every pair of Latin squares in the family is orthogonal.

Definition. Two Latin squares $A,B$ of order $n$ are said to be pseudo orthogonal if for every $(i,j)\in[n]^2$ there exists a $k\in[n]$ such that $$ A(i,k)=B(j,k). $$ A family of Latin squares is called a family of pseudo orthogonal Latin squares (POLS) if every pair of Latin squares in the family is pseudo orthogonal. By [2, Lemma 1], the column $k$ is necessarily unique.

POLS were introduced in [1]. The equivalence between the existence of a family of $m$ POLS and a family of $m$ MOLS follows indirectly from Theorem 1 of [2]. The purpose of the present note is to exhibit a direct bijection between ordered families of $m$ POLS and ordered families of $m$ MOLS.

2. The Main Theorem

Theorem. Fix integers $n\ge1$ and $m\ge2$. Let $\mathcal{M}$ denote the set of all ordered $m$-tuples of mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order $n$, and let $\mathcal{P}$ denote the set of all ordered $m$-tuples of mutually pseudo orthogonal Latin squares of order $n$. For each $t\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$, let $L_t$ and $M_t$ be $n\times n$ arrays related by $$ M_t(a,i)=j \quad\text{if and only if}\quad L_t(i,j)=a. $$ Define $$ f:\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P} $$ by $$ f(L_1,\ldots,L_m)=(M_1,\ldots,M_m). $$ Then $f$ is a bijection.
Proof.

Let $(L_1,\ldots,L_m)\in\mathcal M$. For each $t$, since every symbol occurs exactly once in every row and column of $L_t$, it follows immediately that $M_t$ is a Latin square.

Now let $r\neq s$ and fix rows $a$ and $b$ of $M_r$ and $M_s$. Then $M_r(a,i)=M_s(b,i)=j$ if and only if $L_r(i,j)=a$ and $L_s(i,j)=b$. Thus solutions of $M_r(a,i)=M_s(b,i)$ are in one-to-one correspondence with occurrences of the ordered pair $(a,b)$ in the superposition of $L_r$ and $L_s$. Since $L_r$ and $L_s$ are orthogonal, the ordered pair $(a,b)$ occurs exactly once. Hence $M_r$ and $M_s$ are pseudo orthogonal. Therefore $(M_1,\ldots,M_m)\in\mathcal P$.

Conversely, let $(M_1,\ldots,M_m)\in\mathcal P$. Again, it follows immediately that each $L_t$ is a Latin square. Let $r\neq s$ and fix an ordered pair $(a,b)$. Then $L_r(i,j)=a$ and $L_s(i,j)=b$ if and only if $M_r(a,i)=M_s(b,i)=j$. Thus occurrences of the ordered pair $(a,b)$ in the superposition of $L_r$ and $L_s$ are in one-to-one correspondence with solutions of $M_r(a,i)=M_s(b,i)$. Since $M_r$ and $M_s$ are pseudo orthogonal, there exists a solution. By [2, Lemma 1], this solution is unique. Hence $L_r$ and $L_s$ are orthogonal. Therefore $(L_1,\ldots,L_m)\in\mathcal M$.

The correspondence is clearly its own inverse. Hence it defines a bijection between $\mathcal M$ and $\mathcal P$.

□
Corollary. For every $n$ and $m$, there exist $m$ mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order $n$ if and only if there exist $m$ mutually pseudo-orthogonal Latin squares of order $n$.
Proof. Since $f$ is a bijection between $\mathcal M$ and $\mathcal P$, the set $\mathcal M$ is nonempty if and only if the set $\mathcal P$ is nonempty. □
